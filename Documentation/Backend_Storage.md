# This is how the world works in Chronos

## First :
User logs in for the first time into the server, and is given a random spawn at some random spawn which is mediumly propagated. Not highly populated areas, cuz they might get lost, and obviously not least populated areas because they might feel alone. 
Users can set anchors after they exist in the server for a time. These anchors let the users spawn at the same place every time they log in. Without any anchor, they are given random spawns every time they log in. 
So, when users log into Chronos, they see posts right away. Once they establish their anchors, they spawn at their homes, their artifacts and people passing and tinkering near them. 
And for the anchors part, they can have isolated anchors — I am not the one to decide, cuz it has negligible effect on the server, so it's not my issue. And coming to community anchors and shi, one way we can establish communities is shared anchors, without introducing another structure called "community" — once two or more people have a shared anchor, that's a community. Each person is given only one anchor, and can move it once every 10 days.

## Secondly :
There are portals, 10 to be exact per chunk. How big is a chunk? It's 4320x4320 pixels, so it's 4320 pixels wide. And your scroll distance will be a function of the chunk size and your screen size such that more or less you are 10 scrolls away from one chunk to the other. These portals are scarce, creation of them is first come first served, any user with no moderation on them can create and maintain their portal. You can place your portal anywhere on the map, the portal can take you anywhere on the map (no limits) — as promised, even if some idiot types -99999999999, 99999999 it still accepts, cuz there's just the right tool to satisfy his needs too (virtual chunk materialization). These portals decay — if the portal is not being used by a certain threshold of people for a certain threshold of time, it disappears. It balances the user experience as no weird portals sustain long enough, and gives chance to others to create portals for themselves. Now, what if the portal to where you wanna go doesn't exist in your chunk? Chronos suggests the least time-taking portal hop route to do so, it will be less than 4 hops away. Else it shows such route doesn't exist, and shows you an empty chunk where you can create a new pathway. So you always have a route for your destination. Portals can become landmarks, main rival areas over control. This is a core unique mechanic Chronos introduces — portals and portal hop travel.

## Thirdly :
Posts are the soul of Chronos — they are what make the world inhabited, alive, and worth exploring.

Users can create three types of posts:

**Carvings (Text only)** — rendered using Pretext.js, a zero-dependency JS library that measures and positions multiline text through pure arithmetic with no DOM reflow. Text posts have no border — they float in the world as raw content, like graffiti carved into the space itself. They sit on a subtle dark tint rectangle implied by fill, not drawn by a border. Users can bring their own font via Google Fonts URL or .ttf upload, stored as a user profile property — their voice, their handwriting, persistent across every carving they leave in the world. Username appears bottom left, small and muted, like an artist signing their work. Max width 1080px, height flexible, wraps within that width. Max content 5000 characters.

**Media posts (Images and GIFs)** — images and GIFs placed in the world as contained objects. On upload, the server silently detects aspect ratio and snaps to one of three fixed buckets:
```
Square      — 1080x1080   (1:1)
Portrait    — 1080x1350   (4:5)
Landscape   — 1080x608    (16:9)
```
No error thrown, no friction — the user gets their content looking clean. Each image is stored at 3 LODs generated automatically on upload:
```
LOD 3 (near)    — full bucket dimensions
LOD 2 (medium)  — 1/2 dimensions, ratio preserved
LOD 1 (far)     — 1/4 dimensions, ratio preserved
```
GIFs are compressed to 5 frames or less on average — enough to feel alive, not heavy enough to hurt performance. At LOD 2 GIFs freeze to a thumbnail, only animating at LOD 3 when the user is close enough to care. Media posts carry a thin 1px pastel inner rim in the user's chosen color — the only color on an otherwise gray pixel-stepped border sitting on a dark tint fill.

**Combined posts (Media + Text reply)** — media and text are not inherently bundled. A user can reply to a media post with text, creating a combined post where media sits on top and text sits below. This becomes the standard readable post format but only when a user deliberately adds context to their media. No forced caption fields, no mandatory text. The combination emerges from intent.

**Visual identity** — each user has a single chosen border rim color applied to all their media posts. This becomes spatial identity — regular users are recognized by their rim color before their username. Text posts carry no rim, only the floating tint. At LOD 1 all posts dissolve to flat tint rectangles of their rim color, brightness corresponding to engagement. The world communicates popularity spatially, not numerically — numbers only appear at LOD 3 when you are close enough to read.

All size constraints are enforced silently server side. Anything oversized is downsampled, not rejected. The world stays clean without ever telling a user they did something wrong.

## Fourth :
Interactions in Chronos are designed to feel like marks left on a world, not buttons clicked on an app.

**Personal Map** — every user has a private map of the world, visible only to them. They can drop bookmarks anywhere — on posts, on coordinates, on portal locations — each with a custom icon or emoji of their choosing. Nobody else sees your map. It is your memory of the world, your personal legend, your cartography. A user might mark 🔥 for posts that moved them, ⭐ for anchor communities they respect, 👻 for ghost towns they discovered at the frontier. The personal map is the most intimate feature in Chronos — it turns exploration into a private journal.

**Trails** — every user leaves a trail of their journey through the world, visible only on their personal map. The trail is a Catmull-Rom spline reconstructed at render time from a sparse set of semantically meaningful keypoints — portal hops, anchor visits, long dwells, significant direction changes. Raw movement is never stored. Only meaning is stored. Each keypoint is a coordinate and a timestamp — roughly 20 meaningful events per day for a heavy user, 30 days maximum retention, totaling around 7kb per user. Recent trail segments render brighter, older segments fade toward invisible, beyond 30 days they vanish entirely. Like footprints in sand, not ink on paper. Trails can optionally be shared — exported as a path artifact, a snapshot of a journey someone chose to make public. At that point it becomes a post like anything else, a beautiful curve laid over the world that others can follow or admire.

**Views** — a view is not a click or a scroll-past. A view registers only when a user dwells near a post beyond a time threshold, long enough to have actually seen it. Once per session per post — tracked by JWT. Views are the primary driver of a post's brightness. The world communicates engagement not through numbers but through light — higher views means a brighter pastel rim, growing logarithmically and capped so no post becomes blinding. Numbers only appear at LOD 3, when you are close enough to read. At distance, brightness is the only signal.

**Likes and Dislikes** — binary, generic, no emoji reactions. If you feel something beyond a like, reply with an emoji. That is your expression. The like/dislike mechanic exists to create meaningful signal, not to gamify emotion.

**Threading** — replies are physical. A reply post is placed in the world near the original, connected by a bezier curve running from center to center, bowing away from the cluster of surrounding posts. The curve thickness corresponds to the recency of the reply — newer replies have thicker curves, older ones fade. When all spatial directions around a post are occupied, overflow replies root downward beneath the post in chronological order, connected by a vertical bezier hanging like roots under the earth. At LOD 2 these curves thin to hairlines. At LOD 1 they disappear. But a post with deep tangled threads is visually readable even at distance — the tangle itself is the signal that something significant happened here.

**Flagging** — there are no moderators. No admins. No reddit-style power structures. If a post receives flags beyond a threshold proportional to the chunk's population, it becomes blurred. It still exists at its coordinate. Users can choose to unblur it. The world does not erase things — it dims them.

**Presence indicators** — users are not visible as avatars or cursors moving in real time. But their activity is. If someone is posting near your coordinate, a placeholder appears — "username is posting something nearby" — and dissolves when they are done. You feel inhabited without being watched. Everything communicated publicly through posts. No private conversations. People find their own ways — edges of the universe, whispered coordinates, shared anchor invites. That is their problem to solve beautifully.

**System posts** — certain interactions are not posts and never become posts. Anchor invites, notifications, coordination pings — these are system posts. Structured, ephemeral, functional. No view tracking. No brightness. No threading. They appear like Fortnite party invites or Instagram notifications — clean, purposeful, gone when done. Inviting your friend to your anchor will never accidentally accumulate a million views. The world stays honest.

    How does portal pathfinding work?
Ok so portals are just rows in a table with a from-coordinate and a to-coordinate and which chunk they belong to. That's it. No fancy graph database, no complex structure. When u wanna get somewhere and there's no direct portal, the server just does a BFS — breadth first search — starting from ur current chunk, hopping through portal destinations, stopping at 4 hops or when it finds ur target. If it finds a route it sends u the chain. If it doesn't, it finds the nearest empty chunk and tells u to build a pathway there. The whole thing runs in milliseconds because each step is just a simple lookup by chunk id on an indexed table. Extremely cheap, extremely fast, no infrastructure needed beyond what we already have.
How does PostGIS serve viewport culling?
Posts have a location column — a spatial point representing their center coordinate. PostGIS puts a GIST index on that column which is basically a spatial tree that makes bounding box queries stupid fast. When ur viewport sends x1,y1,x2,y2 to the server, it runs one query — give me all posts whose center falls inside this rectangle — and PostGIS uses that index to answer in under 50ms even with hundreds of thousands of posts. We cap results at 100 posts per query so the response stays lean. That's the whole spatial query system. One index, one query type, covers everything.
How do portals and anchors decay?
There's a cron job running every hour on cron-job.org for free that hits a single endpoint on the FastAPI server. That endpoint runs two queries — delete portals that haven't been used in 7 days below the usage threshold, and clean up trail keypoints older than 30 days. Anchor cooldown isn't a decay thing, it's just enforced at write time — when u try to move ur anchor the server checks when u last moved it and rejects if it's been less than 10 days. One cron job, two queries, handles the entire world's memory management.
Auth and rate limiting?
JWT issued on login, contains ur user id and session id, verified on every request, stateless. No refresh token complexity for MVP. Rate limiting lives in FastAPI middleware in memory — 10 posts per hour, 3 portals per day, 5 flags per hour per user. No Redis needed for MVP since we're on a single instance anyway. Portal creation also checks chunk portal count before inserting — if the chunk already has 10 portals it just rejects. Flag abuse is prevented by a unique constraint in the DB — one flag per user per post, can't game it. When we eventually scale to multiple instances we swap in-memory limiting for Cloudflare rate limiting rules which are free anyway.
How does search work?
Search is intentionally weak and that's a feature not a bug. There's a regions table with names, center coordinates and descriptions. User types "Marvel" and gets back a coordinate. Client pans there. That's the whole search system. U cannot search for individual posts. U cannot search for users by content. Space stays meaningful because u have to actually explore to find things — search only gets u to the front door of a region, not inside someone's living room.
How does personal map storage work?
Bookmarks are just rows with user id, coordinate, emoji and label. Trail keypoints are rows with user id, coordinate, event type and timestamp. Both tables are indexed on user id first because they are never queried by anyone except their owner. The hourly cron job that handles portal decay also deletes trail keypoints older than 30 days in the same run. Storage per user is negligible — a heavy user's entire personal map including 30 days of trail history is under 10kb.
Cold start on free tier?
cron-job.org pings the FastAPI health endpoint every 14 minutes. Render's spin-down timer is 15 minutes of inactivity. So the server never sleeps. Free, takes 2 minutes to set up, never think about it again. The Go WebSocket server on Fly.io doesn't have this problem because persistent WebSocket connections keep it warm naturally.
How does the world get bootstrapped on first deploy?
A seed script runs once. It creates a handful of named regions at known coordinates — Marvel, Anime, Politics, Gaming — plants real pre-written posts in each one that feel like genuine community content, and creates premade portals connecting them. The world has shape and soul from minute one. As real users move in the bot content gets buried naturally under real posts. No one ever knows the difference.
